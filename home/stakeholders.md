# Stakeholders

There are lots of people with vested interests in the prices of drugs. 

- [Pharmaceutical Companies](/home/pharmaceutical-industry): the companies that research, market, and manufacture drugs to prescribed worldwide. They say that they have massive costs and government intervention would severely reduce their income and thus their ability to continue creating lifesaving drugs.

- [Medicare Enrollees](/home/medicare-part-d): some 50 million Americans who receive prescription drugs through medicare. They are older, with fixed incomes generally increasing healthcare needs, and they rely on access to medication through Medicare to keep them health and functional. 

- Private Insurers: these companies administer Medicare Part D and do their own negotiations with drug companies. However, they work under different constraints than the federal government, and costs that these insurers are comfortable with can be too much for the government.

- Providers: hospitals, doctors, and other providers also play a role in managing the cost of these drugs. Their patients have budget constraints, and they are evaluated on their efficiency and cost-effectiveness. They are also the ones who firsthand see the effects of debilitating disease and what money or lack of money can mean for a patient. 

- The government and the taxpayers: we all have a stake in this because we all pay into Medicare. We, too, must balance the continued growth of pharma companies and the drugs they create against the reality that resources are finite and have to be spent wisely. 

- any number of advocacy groups: these groups advocate for their stakeholders. Lobbyists for pharma companies try to convince us that the companies will fail if they aren't allowed to continue making enormous profits. Patient groups advocate for continued access to cutting edge drugs and new treatments. And consumer and fiscal advocates see the continually increasing costs and say that something must be done to bring costs under control.
