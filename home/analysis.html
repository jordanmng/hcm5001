Is negotiating prices a good thing?

The pharmaceutical industry is many things: it creates lifesaving drugs, it makes a lot of money, it is a powerful force in international affairs, and it is also an industry where we (the United States) have a huge comparative advantage. We train the best scientists from around the world, many of whom stay here and contribute to science and medicine.

The problem with pharmaceutical pricing seems to be structural. The cost to create these drugs is enormous and there are always challenges in proving the safety and efficacy a drug. The physicians who prescribe the drugs and the pharmacists who provide the drugs are in high demand and their labor increases the costs that are baked into the reimbursements that the hospitals and drug makers receive from Medicare.

So these drugs will probably remain very-to-extremely expensive to create and prescribe, and yet preventing people from accessing a lifesaving medication because of their inability to pay is not acceptable. Healthcare should be a human right, and that means giving everyone the best care that they can get, regardless of their ability to pay. 

There are many angles to a solution in this area, but no silver bullet without an impossible amount of political overhaul. We of course must negotiate pricing and not encourage drug companies to gouge the federal government with various restrictions on Medicare. We should also recognize that the government funds most basic research and most scientific discovery stands on the shoulders of that research (though arguments about positive externalities become relevant here). And we also must recognize that we live in a capitalist world, so a utopian allocation of resources is probably not realistic - it is probably not possible to give maximum care to every patient, but we should be optimizing the care received as much as possible. In the realm of prescription drugs, this means encouraging or requiring generics.

There are also lots of policy choices outside healthcare that we can make to improve health. We can increase taxes on the rich and reduce military spending and direct that money toward more healthcare and medical research. We can also reduce incarceration, which costs a lot and is a drag on the health and GDP of the country. We also need to increase the base level of health in the country. Many of the drugs that cost Medicare the most are the drugs that treat diabetes and cardiovascular disease, two extremely prevalent diseases that are mitigated by good health practices and more primary care. These are, of course, all pretty lofty goals, and I doubt many of these choices, either political or individual, will be made in the next 50 years. But something has to change, because the current cycle of spiraling costs is unsustainable. 

 

One specific policy recommendation that I've seen (but cannot find a source for so maybe I'm making this up) is removing exclusivity for a drug once it recoups its development costs and makes some profit, capped at 150%, 200%, or similar. This is an interesting idea and would help generics come on the market faster to reduce costs, but the fact remains that the pharmaceutical industry relies on its blockbusters to fund its business, so cutting potential profits has a real risk of reducing the number of drugs discovered each year.
