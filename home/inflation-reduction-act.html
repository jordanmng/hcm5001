The Inflation Reduction Act

 

The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) is a sweeping bill that aims to kick-start green energy manufacturing and technology, invest in rebuilding infrastructure, overhaul corporate tax law, increase funding for the IRS, and, most importantly for us, lower prescription drug costs by allowing Medicare to negotiate the price it pays for some drugs. 

These negotiations will happen gradually and result in a slowly-but-surely growing list of drugs that have had their prices negotiated down. There are several rounds of negotiation and detailed guidance on what drugs can be selected and how negotiations should happen, but they boil down to: 1) will this drug continue to be dominant in its market? and 2) how much did this drug cost to develop? Initial savings from the list of prices to be implemented in 2026 suggest that there could be significant savings, around 50% or more per drug, which will result in billions of dollars saved per year. 

Another much less-lauded portion of the IRA is the requirement that manufacturers return money to Medicare in the form of a rebate if their drug prices rise faster than inflation. Given that drug prices and spending were and are regularly outpacing GDP growth and inflation, this is certainly a step in the right direction. It does not do much to increase access to medication but it slows down the rate at which medications get more expensive, which means that drugs will remain at their current level of affordability for longer. 

These two factors together are projected to save Medicare $280 billion dollars.1
